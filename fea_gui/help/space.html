Space structures (ECSS)

Margins of safety of small metallic, bolted space structures (CubeSat, microsatellite) against a requirement set that you enter and confirm. Open it with Tools → Space structures (ECSS)… or with Margins of safety (ECSS)… in the Results step.

The program ships no standard number. It contains no safety factor, no launcher load level and no correlation limit, and it quotes no text of a standard. The template only names the fields and the clauses to fill. You enter every value from the documents of your project (your own copy of the standard, the launcher user's guide, the material handbook), write down where it comes from, and confirm the set. Until then the verdict is UNCONFIRMED_REQUIREMENTS and never PASS.

Margin of safety

For every part, load state and check the dialog computes

MoS = allowable / (J · σ) − 1

  • σ – equivalent stress of the unfactored load (von Mises, maximum principal or maximum shear, chosen in the rule) at the governing place.
  • J – the design factor: the product of the factors that you list in the rule (Factors of J), for the structure class and the verification approach of the set. The formula chain of the standard is yours to enter: which factors multiply, is the decision of the person who confirms the set.
  • allowable – Fy (yield) or Fu (ultimate) of the material, or the shear values Fsy, Fsu; for a buckling check the lowest buckling load factor λ of the buckling analysis (MoS = λ / J − 1).

The governing place is the highest-stress place that is not a singularity (clamped edge, sharp inner corner, point force, bolt connection – the same classification as in Results). A place influenced by a boundary condition counts (conservative). Singular places are listed separately.

Procedure

  1. Requirement set tab: New from ECSS template, choose the verification approach (qualification model, protoflight, analysis only), type the factor values with document, revision and clause, and fill the margin rules: allowable, stress measure, the factors that make up J, and how thermal stress is factored in a combined case (same as mechanical, separate with its own factors, none).
  2. Type your name into Confirmed by and press Confirm requirement set. The program checks that every used factor has a value and a source. Any later edit withdraws the confirmation. An imported file is never confirmed; whoever uses it confirms it again.
  3. Material allowables tab: A or B basis values from the material handbook (Fy, Fu, Fsy, Fsu in MPa, temperature, source), then Confirm selected material. Without confirmed A / B allowables the margin uses Re / Rm of the material and is only indicative.
  4. Solve the study, then Compute margins of safety in the Margins of safety tab. A click on a row marks the place in the 3D view. Structure class of the parts: metallic (default), joints / inserts, composite (composite has no stress-based check: it is never assessed).
  5. Singularity justification table: every singular place needs a note of yours (why it is acceptable) before sign-off. The note is stored with the place and found again after re-meshing (same part, within the tolerance).
  6. The Word report gets a chapter Space structures: margins of safety with the requirement set and its provenance, the allowables, the lowest margins and the singularity table.

Verdict

VerdictMeaning
PASSThe requirement set, the rules and the allowables of every part are confirmed, the analysis is linear, and every margin outside singularities is not negative.
FAILA margin with confirmed inputs is negative.
UNCONFIRMED_REQUIREMENTSSomething is not confirmed or complete (set, rule, allowable, basis not A / B, structure class without a rule, nonlinear analysis, all highest places singular, a state with mechanical and thermal loads that cannot be factored separately). The numbers are shown, but they are indicative.

Sign-off additionally needs every singular place justified. The factors scale the stress, which is valid only for linear studies; for a nonlinear analysis apply the factors to the loads and solve again.

Combined mechanical and thermal loads

Each load state is mechanical or thermal by its loads. A case can combine states (Combinations of the study, or cases defined in the project): the design stress is Jmech·σmech + Jthermal·σthermal as the rule says. A single state that contains both kinds of loads cannot be separated: the larger factor is used and the result is not PASS – split it into two load cases.

With same as mechanical (and likewise separate and none) the factors apply to additive thermal loads too, but a relieving thermal load is not credited with the factor: ECSS-E-ST-32-10C Rev.2 Corr.1, 4.3.2.1 b and c, let a relieving load count with a factor of 1,0 or less. Whether a thermal stress relieves or adds is not known place by place, so the margin takes the largest of the three combinations Jm·σmech + Jt·σthermal, Jm·σmech + 1,0·σthermal (relieving thermal load) and 1,0·σmech + Jt·σthermal (relieving mechanical load), each extra one only where the factor replaced by 1,0 is above 1. This holds for every thermal handling: Jt = Jm for same as mechanical, the product of the listed factors for separate and 1,0 for none.

Where the factors come from (ECSS-E-ST-32-10C)

The program contains no factor value, but the structure of the template follows ECSS-E-ST-32-10C Rev.2 Corrigendum 1 (1 August 2019); the clauses to look the values up in:

  • Design factor for loads (4.1.4): KM model factor (4.1.4.2), KP project factor (4.1.4.3), KQ qualification test factor of satellites (4.1.4.4), which is used only at system level (4.2.1 d). Table 4-1 gives Coef A = KQ·KP·KM (satellite) and Coef B / C = FOSY / FOSU times KLD (times KMP for launch vehicles).
  • Additional factors (4.1.5): KLD local design factor (4.1.5.2: for example joints and inserts, holes, welds), KMP margin policy factor of launch vehicles (4.1.5.3). The acceptance factor KA belongs to test loads (Table 4-2) and is not part of J.
  • Values: test factors Table 4-2, metallic / FRP / sandwich parts Table 4-3 (FOSY, FOSU, and the higher values of a verification by analysis only), joints, inserts and connections Table 4-4, buckling Table 4-5. Values are to be agreed with the customer (4.1.3). Table 4-4 note a: these factors are not applied to the bolt preload (see Bolts).
  • Thermal loads: 4.3.2.1 b / c for satellites (factors apply to additive loads, 1,0 or less for relieving ones), 4.3.2.1 d and 4.3.2.2 b for launch vehicles (1,0 for thermally induced loads).

The old template row KT is no longer listed: KT occurs in the standard only in the informative Annex A (correction of the launch-vehicle qualification factor for a thermal gradient). A set that still has such a row keeps it (any factor name is accepted); KMP replaces it in the template.

Launch loads (stage S2)

The Launch environment and Launch loads tabs of the same dialog. The program contains no launcher or standard value: environments, quasi-static factors and budgets are entered by you with their source and confirmed by you; an edit after the confirmation withdraws it.

  1. Launch environment: choose the launch axis and the QSL combination, the modal damping ratio with its source (part of the confirmation), then for each level (qualification, protoflight, acceptance) type the sine table (frequency, amplitude in g, sweep rate), the random ASD (g2/Hz, duration per axis, an optional sigma multiple of the RMS stress) and the shock SRS (g), each with document, revision and clause. Press Confirm environments.
  2. Limits of the requirement set (same tab): Add the empty S2 limit rows, then enter the QSL factors (qsl_axial_g, qsl_lateral_g, optional qsl_lateral_2_g), the mass and centre-of-mass budgets and the minimum natural frequencies, each with its source. They are confirmed together with the requirement set.
  3. Generate QSL load cases makes the signed quasi-static cases (QSL +X, QSL -Y +Z, ...): every axis alone with both signs, and the combinations the QSL combination asks for. The body force is the factor times g. Solve the study to get their states; the margins assess them like any other state. Limits of the generator: one axial factor is used for both signs; the lateral directions are the two global axes that are not the launch axis; axial_both_laterals applies both lateral factors at once, so the lateral resultant is √2 times the factor when both are equal (use axial_lateral when the requirement gives a resultant); a factor of 0 or below is refused. The status of the cases is current only while a regeneration gives the same names and gravity vectors - an edited or deleted case, or a changed factor, axis or combination, makes it non-current.
  4. Launch loads tab: Run 3-axis analysis applies the chosen environment along X, Y and Z over one modal basis (the dynamics settings: number of modes, damping) and shows the per-axis maxima, the envelope (maximum over the axes of |U| and von Mises, with the governing axis; the fatigue damage is added over the axes because they are tested one after the other on the same hardware, the largest damage of one axis is listed for information) and the envelope by part. A click on a part row marks the place in the 3D view. For random vibration the stress is the RMS (1 sigma); it enters the margins only as sigma times RMS when you entered sigma. Margins with envelopes adds the envelopes to the margins of the solved states; the verdict of the margins tab, of the MCP tool and of the report goes through the envelopes whenever a current 3-axis result exists. A result is current only while the study, the mesh, the damping and the environments are those it was computed with (the study name, the thread count and static load cases that the dynamics does not use, such as the generated QSL cases, do not count). After a change that does count, the verdict is UNCONFIRMED with the reason "3-axis result is out of date - re-run"; it never falls back to a static PASS. The damping is taken from the environments (shown with its source); without it the damping of the study is used and no margin can be PASS. A run of fewer than three axes or of fewer kinds than the level has, a random table without sigma, and any mode below the lowest frequency of the shock table (it would respond with zero, whatever its share of the mass) also forbid a PASS; the modes are listed and the blocker is removed by extending the table down to cover them.
  5. Mass, centre of mass and budgets: total mass, centre of mass and the inertia tensor about it (mesh, point masses with their rotary inertia, surface masses; parallel-axis shifted), compared with your budgets, and the first axial and lateral natural frequency against your minimum frequencies. Your limit f_min_eff_mass_fraction is the share of the effective mass from which a single mode counts as significant in a direction (without it every mode counts, so the lowest mode of the model decides); the item shows the lowest significant mode per direction. A computed mode below the minimum frequency whose share reaches the fraction is a FAIL. The item is UNCONFIRMED (the reason is written next to it) when the computed modes do not reach the minimum frequency, when they account for less than 90 % of the effective mass of a direction, or when modes below the minimum frequency carry more than 10 % of it without one of them reaching the fraction (split mass); it is PASS only when the computed modes cover the minimum frequency and no significant mode lies below it. The 90 % level is an analysis constant of the program, not a requirement. The basis is taken to hold the lowest modes of the model. The frequency items need the confirmed launch environment (the launch axis decides which limit applies). The rotary inertia of a point mass is part of the inertia tensor only for a rigid coupling (the solver keeps only the translation of a distributing one).

Results that rest on an unconfirmed environment or requirement set are labelled UNCONFIRMED_INPUTS, their margins are never PASS, and a budget item shows PASS or FAIL only with a confirmed requirement set and a limit with a source. The Word report gets the subsection Launch loads, mass properties and budgets. MCP: fea_space_env_set, fea_space_qsl_generate, fea_space_run_3axis, fea_space_mass_budget.

Test correlation (vibration test)

Open Tools → Test correlation (ECSS)…. The dashboard compares the natural modes and the responses of the model with a vibration test: MAC of the paired modes, relative frequency error, CoMAC per sensor DOF, and the peak frequency shift, the peak amplitude error [dB] and the RMS error of the response curves. Every metric is judged only against a limit of your confirmed requirement set (the corr_* rows; Add limit rows to requirement set adds them empty). A limit that is missing, has no source, or belongs to an unconfirmed (or edited) set makes its rows UNCONFIRMED. The dashboard has no field for a limit and no way to confirm anything.

Relation to ECSS-E-ST-32-11C (31 July 2008): the MAC (3.2.26), the CoMAC (3.2.7) and the frequency difference are computed as the standard defines them (the CoMAC with every mode shape normalised to unit length first, so that it does not depend on how the two mode sets are scaled). The standard makes the MAC, the (cross-)orthogonality, the modulus difference and the frequency difference mandatory (Table 5-2, 5.8.1.1 b; the CoMAC is not mandatory). The program computes the mass-weighted cross-orthogonality COC and the auto-orthogonality AOC of the test modes (3.2.39, 5.7.1.4, 5.8.1.3) and a modulus difference - see Orthogonality and modulus difference below; without a reduced mass their rows are UNCONFIRMED, never PASS; the SEREP reduction the program computes from the FE modes is indicative only (its rows are UNCONFIRMED whatever the value) - a PASS or FAIL needs the imported mass matrix of the test-analysis model (TAM). The example criteria of Table 5-3 are not normative and differ by class of mode (fundamental bending modes, modes with more than 10 % of the effective mass, other modes); the program has one limit per metric that you enter for all modes, and no limit of the standard is built in.

Import

  • Import test data… reads a CSV or a UFF file (dataset 58 functions, 55 mode shapes; 2411 / 15 / 2420 coordinate systems; 164 units) on a worker thread. A truncated or malformed file is refused as a whole; nothing is executed.
  • Sensors tab: where each test node is in the model (a point in mm, a probe or a surface selection). Test nodes without a sensor are left out and named.
  • Functions tab: for every measured FRF / PSD the frame (absolute, or relative to the base), the normalisation (none, per base acceleration, per force), the unit and, for a controller PSD without node and direction, the probe and DOF. A UFF 58 states the numerator unit in record 9 and the denominator unit in record 10: g and N make a g/N function. The model curve is built in the quantity, frame and normalisation of the function; a different quantity, a missing frame choice or an unknown unit makes the rows UNCONFIRMED, never PASS.
  • Units are read case-aware: mN is a millinewton and MN a meganewton, Mg a megagram, mg a milli-g. An underscore is no operator, and ms2 is refused as ambiguous (m/s2 or ms squared): write m/s^2. The damping of a UFF 55 has no unit in the file: a value above 0.2 is refused unless you choose ratio or percent before the import. The eigenvalue of a complex mode (UFF 55, analysis type 3) is assumed to be in rad/s: a file written in Hz gives a frequency 2π too low, so check the frequencies.

Local sensor axes (UFF 2420)

The 3 × 3 part of a dataset 2420 does not say whether its columns or its rows are the local axes. The import takes the columns (the convention of OpenModal, the writer of a public sample file); choose rows in Local sensor axes when your test system writes them the other way. A symmetric matrix does not depend on the choice. For a file with rotated nodes the data at those nodes stay UNCONFIRMED until you tick Convention confirmed for this file (checked against the sensor directions of your test). The confirmation is stored with the file's SHA-256 and the convention, is withdrawn when you change the convention, and shows who gave it (GUI, or an agent through MCP – check that one yourself). A node whose system is not fully known (cylindrical, spherical, or defined only in the old dataset 18, which is not interpreted) is left out and named; the displacement system 1 without a dataset 2420 is taken as global, with a note.

Orthogonality and modulus difference

COC = |φtestT M φFE| / √((φtestT M φtest)(φFET M φFE)) (3.2.39; not squared, unlike the MAC; the magnitude is shown because the sign of a mode shape is arbitrary). AOC is the same between test modes (5.7.1.4: the standard wants its off-diagonal elements below 10 %). The standard asks for the mass matrix reduced to the measured DOFs (5.8.1.3 b) and allows any reduction (Guyan, IRS, dynamic reduction, SEREP; 6.2). The list Mass of COC / AOC chooses where it comes from:

  • SEREP of the FE modes (default, indicative only): the program computes it from the compared FE modes at the sensor DOFs and the mass of the study (the consistent mass of the bodies, surface masses and point masses), Ma = (Φa+)T G Φa+ with G = ΦTMΦ. It needs a tet10 mesh, at least two compared FE modes and more sensor DOFs than compared FE modes (measure more DOFs or add sensors; leaving FE modes out is no remedy: the COC would then be that of the projection onto the remaining modes) and sensors that see every compared mode; if not, the checks are not computed and the reason is shown. Guyan (static) reduction is not offered: it needs the stiffness matrix and sensors at mesh nodes. The FE modes are exactly orthogonal in Ma by construction, so only the COC and AOC of the test modes carry information. Every COC / AOC row of this route is UNCONFIRMED - never PASS and never FAIL - with the reason indicative (SEREP reduction from the FE modes) - a PASS needs a test-analysis-model (TAM) mass matrix from the test lab (ECSS-E-ST-32-11C 5.8.1.3 b, 6.3): the number is shown in the table, the matrix and the report, but it is not judged. A value outside the limit is shown as a warning hint, and the row stays UNCONFIRMED: an indicative number must not make a verdict either way. The overall verdict is therefore never PASS while COC / AOC rows come from this route (unless you declare the criterion not applicable with its document, revision and clause). Why: see below.
  • Imported matrix (TAM): Import mass matrix… reads a CSV of the test-analysis model: a header ,N1:x,N1:y,… (the test node labels and x / y / z), then one row per DOF starting with the same label; an optional line # unit: kg. The matrix must be symmetric and positive semi-definite (at most 2000 DOFs; the null space is told from the mass by the noise floor of the file: the reader knows how many digits every number was printed with and takes the rounding bound of the printed digits (half a unit of the last digit of every cell, the Frobenius norm of those bounds; by Weyl no eigenvalue of the file moves by more than that) as the lowest floor, besides 10 times the most negative eigenvalue, kept between 1e-8 and 1e-4 of the largest one; an eigenvalue within the band counts as null space. A singular matrix written with 6 - 8 significant digits (%.6g ... %.8g) therefore loads as singular, also when the rounding left no negative eigenvalue (before, a singular matrix of 5 DOFs and 4 modes written with %.6g was read as full rank in about half of the cases, the null-space hold did not fire and the correlation could end in PASS). The price: an eigenvalue that is genuinely below that floor (a mass spread beyond about 1e5 at 6 digits) is read as null space too - the rows are then held, never passed - while a full-rank matrix with a spread up to about 1e5 written with 6 digits is read as full rank. A file whose longest number has at most three significant digits is taken as exact (hand-written round numbers). A negative eigenvalue beyond 1e-4 of the largest one and beyond that floor is refused). Only this route can PASS or FAIL the COC / AOC rows, its DOFs must be exactly the sensor DOFs of the correlation and it must be written in the global axes of the FE model (the test shapes are rotated into them): with rotated sensors (UFF 2420) the COC / AOC rows stay UNCONFIRMED until you tick TAM in global axes. The COC does not depend on the scale of the matrix. The off-diagonal terms of the FE modes through the imported matrix are reported as a check of the TAM: above 0.10 (the example of Table 5-3) the matrix does not fit the model and the rows are UNCONFIRMED (the check of 6.3.2 b, TAM modes against reference-model modes, needs the stiffness matrix of the TAM and is not done). The generalised masses of the FE modes through the matrix, diag(ΦaT MTAM Φa), must equal those of the study, diag(ΦT M Φ), up to one global factor (the unit of the file): a deviation above 10 % (the size of the off-diagonal tolerance; an error of 10 % in a diagonal term moves the normalisation of a COC by about 5 %) holds the rows - this catches a payload left out of the matrix whose mass the compared modes move, or modal masses rescaled mode by mode. An FE mode that the matrix does not see (zero energy at the sensor DOFs) holds the rows too. What no check of the program can verify: a matrix that reproduces the orthogonality and the generalised masses of the FE modes can still be wrong for other shapes (a payload that the compared modes hardly move; a SEREP matrix padded with a small regularisation is blind to everything outside the FE modes). The mass fidelity of the TAM is the responsibility of the test laboratory. A matrix whose rank is below its size (a SEREP matrix exported by another program is of this kind) gives the part of a test shape in its null space no mass at all: that share is shown in the same column as the SEREP residual, and the rows of a mode with more than 0.05 there are UNCONFIRMED (all rows are, when a compared FE mode lies there). With only one FE mode compared the fit of the matrix to the model cannot be checked (no off-diagonal term; a rank-1 matrix would give COC = 1 to any shape) and the rows are UNCONFIRMED. The matrix belongs to the study it was imported for and to the state of the model it was imported for (densities, added masses, mesh): another state holds the rows, and a stored result is OUTDATED when the density, the added masses or the mesh changed after the correlation. The box TAM in global axes is cleared at every import, and a correlation that is given a new matrix without options does not take over the stored statement; the statement is stored with who made it (GUI, or an agent through MCP - check that one yourself). The file is read like the test data (bounded, UNC paths refused); it is not stored in the study, only its fingerprint (the result is UNVERIFIABLE until you import it again).
  • Not computed: the rows are UNCONFIRMED.

Why the SEREP COC is indicative only. The reduced mass is exact only for shapes in the span of the compared FE modes at the sensors, so the COC of a test mode is the COC of its projection onto that span (q = Φa+ψ): the squares of a row of the COC matrix add up to 1 over the compared modes, and a test shape that matches no FE mode can still show a COC close to 1 with the nearest combination. Four rounds of review with real ccx models found no rule by which the program, from the FE modes alone, can tell when that number is the COC of the shape: a test mode with a part in a lower FE mode outside the compared window gave 0.9957 through the window and 0.80 through a wider basis (heavy tip mass; real tet10 cantilever, FE modes 3 - 10 compared, test mode 0.8 φ4 + 0.6 φ1); a shape error at a heavy point that the basis hardly moves is small in the Euclidean residual and large in the COC of the full mass matrix (8 masses, two of 1000 at the free end, the tip 10 % off: r = 0.003, SEREP COC 1.00, full-mass COC 0.52); uniaxial sensors resolve only some of the modes; the choice of the basis (the window, or every resolvable solved mode) changes the number, and no guard that the program can compute settles it. So the SEREP COC / AOC are informative: they are computed and shown with the out-of-span residual r = 1 − |Φaq|² / |ψ|² of every test mode (Euclidean norm over the sensor DOFs; 0 = inside the span; a warning above 0.05, which does not bound the mass-weighted error either), with a warning when the sensors separate the compared modes badly (condition number of the mass-normalised sensor matrix above 100; not computed above 1e8) or when the mass of the study is not the mass the modes were solved with (the FE modes are not mass-orthogonal within 1e-3, e.g. a density edited after the solution) - but they are never judged. A PASS or FAIL of the COC / AOC needs the mass matrix of the test-analysis model (TAM) from the test laboratory (ECSS-E-ST-32-11C 5.8.1.3 b, 6.3): import it below. The MAC, the frequency difference and the modulus difference are not affected.

The limits are the requirement-set rows corr_coc_diag_min, corr_coc_offdiag_max, corr_aoc_offdiag_max and corr_modulus_diff_max. The Limits tab and the report print what the standard says as a hint (Table 5-3, not normative: COC diagonal terms > 0,90, off-diagonal terms < 0,10; 5.7.1.4 b: AOC off-diagonal below 10 %); no value is a default. The pairing stays the MAC pairing; the off-diagonal COC is taken among the paired modes. A test mode with a DOF that was not measured has no COC / AOC (its rows are UNCONFIRMED). The modulus difference is named in Table 5-2 and the standard gives no formula: the program scales both shapes to unit length on the measured DOFs and reports the largest | |test| − |FE| | as a fraction of the largest test amplitude, with the DOF where it occurs. This is the program's own definition, not the standard's; judge it with a limit you can justify.

Verdict

VerdictMeaning
PASSEvery judged row passed against confirmed limits and nothing was left out.
FAILA judged row failed, or a test mode has no FE partner.
UNCONFIRMED_REQUIREMENTSA limit, a comparison, the requirement set or the rotation convention is not confirmed.
PARTIAL_SCOPEEvery judged row passed, but part of the test was left out, and the dashboard lists what: test modes outside the frequency window, test nodes without a sensor, measured DOFs that the sensors do not map (rotational DOFs are never compared), functions that were not compared, and test resonances outside the compared band (a response band, or a model curve shorter than the measurement, hides them; they are named with their frequency). A resonance is a peak that stands at least hidden_peak_db (6 dB) above its surroundings: a detection setting, not a limit of a standard.
OUTDATEDThe stored result no longer matches the study: the limits, the sensors, the function setups, the damping table, the test data (file, content, rotation convention), the FE modes or the dynamics responses changed after it was computed.
UNVERIFIABLEThe stored result cannot be checked now: the solved results or the test data it was computed from are not at hand (a model edit discards the results; a re-opened project has no test data). It is never shown as PASS: import the file, solve and correlate again.

The result is a stored record of an earlier computation. Its provenance (a fingerprint of every input) is tamper-evident, not a recomputation and not a signature: it shows that the inputs are unchanged, it does not prove the numbers. Row statuses are recomputed from the stored values and the current limits; to verify the numbers themselves recompute (press Correlate). The report prints the same verified verdict, the current confirmation state of the requirement set, what was verified and what was not.

Criteria declared not applicable

A criterion that the requirement set declares not applicable (with document, revision and clause) is not judged. Because that can hide a failing check, the Limits tab shows every corr_* limit read-only (value, unit, not applicable, source, status), the line above the table names every not-applicable criterion, and the Space structures dialog lists them before you confirm the set. The result, the MCP reply and the report list each one with who set the requirement set (confirmed by, GUI or MCP) and through which interface the flag itself was entered (MCP – an agent, to be checked by you; an imported file; the GUI).

Measured damping and automation

Write measured damping into the dynamics puts the measured modal damping of the paired modes into the damping table of the dynamics analysis; its results are then discarded (solve again) and the stored result is tied to that table. MCP: fea_space_test_import (with rotation, rotation_confirmed – only when you ask for it –, damping_unit, sensors, functions) and fea_space_correlate.

Model quality checks

Before the margins are used, the finite element model itself must be checked (ECSS-E-ST-32-11C 6.4.1 and common FEM practice). Open Model quality checks... in the Space structures dialog. The seven checks run in the background on copies of the study (the study and its results do not change):

#CheckWhat it shows
1Mass and centre of massFE mass and centre against your budget (Budgets tab), the CAD volumes of the parts, and the mass as the solver sees it (unit-gravity statics).
2Free-free modesWith every support removed there must be exactly six rigid-body modes without strain energy; more means that a part is not connected - the parts that move independently are named.
3Unit enforced displacementThree unit translations and three small rotations of the launch interface: no strain energy and no net reaction; a stray support or spring that holds the model is named.
4Isothermal expansionA uniform temperature change must not cause stress (finds hidden over-constraints and a wrong expansion-coefficient unit).
5Effective modal massCumulative effective mass in x, y, z up to the cutoff frequency; a truncated modal basis is named.
6Mesh convergenceThe first natural frequencies and the governing stress on finer meshes (slow; the check box can leave it out, the verdict is then INCOMPLETE).
7EquilibriumForces and moments of the solved states, recomputed from the stored reactions.

The table is a traffic light: green PASS, amber WARN, red FAIL, grey UNCONFIRMED or SKIPPED. The tolerances are not in the program. They are limits of the requirement set (Tolerances tab: value, document, revision, clause); a limit counts only while the requirement set is confirmed, and an edit withdraws the confirmation. A check without a confirmed limit shows its numbers but is UNCONFIRMED, never PASS; only physical invariants (six rigid-body modes of a free body, equilibrium to the tolerance of the solver) can fail without a limit. A check that cannot run (for example check 3 and 4 need the direct solver module) is SKIPPED, and fewer than seven finished checks give the verdict INCOMPLETE. The run is kept for the Word report (chapter Model quality checks); if the study or the limits change afterwards (also while the dialog stays open), the table, the verdict and the report say outdated / OUT OF DATE instead of showing the old verdict, and a newly opened project starts without a kept run. The dialog keeps the ticks of the launch interface when it is closed and opened again.

A setting never weakens a check silently. Every budget and numerical setting that differs from the default is printed next to the check it changes (dialog, MCP answer, report: table Budgets and settings that differ from the defaults, with the number of the check). What cannot give a full PASS: check 1 without the solver cross-check of the mass or with a wider plausibility range of the densities, check 3 with fewer than the six unit states (none = SKIPPED) or with an interface that names every support of the study (a stray support is then declared part of the interface and cannot be found: name only the launch interface), check 4 with a wider range of the expansion coefficient, check 6 with fewer levels (the default is three), a smaller element-size ratio of the levels (the default is 1.5) or fewer frequencies (the default is five): these are UNCONFIRMED with the reason, they are never a PASS. Check 6 needs an element-size ratio of the levels from 1.3 to 4 (a numerical setting: meshes that differ by less cannot be judged; a smaller one is SKIPPED, in MCP an error). Every number of the MCP options is bounded. The check time is printed per check, so a report that merges a re-run of one check with the others shows when each one was run.

Check 7 recomputes the balance of every static state from its stored reactions. The relative imbalance is divided by the resultant max(|applied force|, |reactions|) when the state has a net applied force, and the same for the moment (the report lists the divisor of every state): a 1 % imbalance of a 100 N load is 0.01 whatever the slenderness of the model, it is not diluted by the sum of the nodal force norms of the solver (for a cantilever at L/h = 60 that sum is almost 200 times the load). Only a self-equilibrated state (a pure moment, a uniform temperature change, a bolt preload) has no resultant to divide by; it is judged on the sum of the nodal norms that the solver recorded with the state. A result saved by an older version has no such record: its self-equilibrated states are then UNCONFIRMED ("recompute with this version"), never a guessed verdict. Supports that hold the same node and direction (two symmetry planes that both fix it) count that node more than once in their per-support reactions; the solver records this overlap, and the per-support reactions are judged as the sum minus the overlap. Every support must have its reaction in the result, and a reaction that was changed, zeroed or lost is a violation. A result without the recorded overlap is judged on the total of the record and is never a full PASS. A state whose reactions or residual vectors are missing is a violation (also when its name is no load case of the study), never a pass. Two numerical settings of the check (not limits of a standard) are printed with its numbers: consistency_tolerance (how closely the recomputed and the recorded residual must agree) and net_load_share (the share of the nodal norms of the applied load below which its resultant counts as rounding noise, i.e. the state as self-equilibrated).

The requirement set behind the limits is shown with who confirmed it and through which interface: a confirmation made through MCP (an agent) is amber in the dialog and in the report and must be checked and confirmed by you in the Space structures dialog.

Structural Analysis Report (ECSS)

The report that a structural verification hands to the customer. Choose Structural Analysis Report (ECSS) instead in the Word report dialog (the box is available when the study has space settings); in MCP the tool is fea_space_sar_report. It does not repeat the analyses: every chapter is written by the stage that owns it, in the ECSS order.

The sign-off verdict. The summary at the beginning gives every chapter a status and one overall verdict. It is PASS only when every included chapter is current and PASS; anything outdated, unconfirmed, partial, not run or unverifiable gives NOT_SIGNED_OFF (a failed check or a negative margin gives FAIL). A stored result that the chapter reports as out of date is never turned into PASS. A build without the S4 module gives NOT_SIGNED_OFF (the interfaces and bolts were not assessed); the optional chapter without a stored correlation does not block. The configuration chapter is UNVERIFIABLE without a geometry file hash and WARN when the setup digest covers the study only. When several environment levels have dynamic tables, only the level with a current 3-axis result is assessed and the others give PARTIAL_SCOPE. The scope of the report is named in the Scope line. Not saved in the project file, so a report made from a reopened project says not run: the model quality checks and the 3-axis analysis must be run again in the session. The verdict is bookkeeping of the program, not the approval by the responsible engineer. Only local paths are removed from what you typed (author, company, names, sources of the requirements); the footer and the properties of the file are cleaned too.

Software Validation Dossier (python -m verification.dossier --out dossier.docx): a document made automatically from the stored results of verification.run_public and the public catalogue: only references that do not come from the program (closed-form, handbook, literature, MYSTRAN); the program's own cross-checks are listed separately and never counted as validation. The private cross-check never enters (any key or value of the results that names it is refused), the output is deterministic (without the run metadata the date reads date unknown); a case that lacks only its MYSTRAN comparison is reported as such, not as outside tolerance.

Reference benchmark (python -m verification.cubesat_benchmark --out folder): a CubeSat-class aluminium frame with four bolted panels, an orthotropic PCB with a surface mass and a payload point mass, built from code. It runs the chain statics, launch loads, model checks, correlation and the report, and compares the natural frequencies of the direct solver (rslab) with CalculiX. Every number in it is an illustrative test value (not ECSS, not a launcher); the mesh convergence check is left out to keep the run short, so its report is correctly NOT_SIGNED_OFF.

Interface forces, notching, bolts, PCB and rails (tab Interfaces, bolts, PCB)

Nothing on these pages is a standard value. Every limit, factor and convention is a row of the requirement set (page Limits: value, unit, document, revision, clause) and counts only after you confirm the set. A number typed into a calculation page is used but never confirmed: the verdict stays UNCONFIRMED_REQUIREMENTS.

  • Interface forces – the base reaction of the held nodes (constraints and elastic supports) for a base excitation: the modal base-reaction vectors per mode, the force and moment spectrum of a sine profile and the RMS force and moment of a random ASD. The force is the support force of Newton's law: the elastic reaction plus the damping force of the modes (at resonance m·a·√(1 + 4ζ²)/(2ζ) for one mass); only the held components of a held node count (sliding and cylindrical supports; a node held by two constraints is shared between their groups), and a model held only by elastic supports works. The program checks that the reaction of a unit base acceleration at f → 0 equals the total mass times the acceleration. The moment is taken about the centroid of the held nodes unless you give a point. Damping is an uncertain input (±50 %).
  • Notching – the input profile is multiplied by min(1, limit / (J · predicted quantity)); on a PSD limit (random) the factor acts as J². The limited quantity is the interface force or moment, the acceleration at a probe or a stress (sine), or the force PSD (random). The minimum notch width and the floor of a notch come from the requirement set. A non-finite or negative input, J ≤ 0 or an absurd width is an error, never a verdict; a limit table applies its smallest value outside its range. The probe and stress limits need a harmonic result of one base direction (a force excitation is refused) and hold at that probe only. The breakpoint table never exceeds the limit: it is simplified from below (within 0.1 dB, never above the profile), re-evaluated between the analysis frequencies on a grid up to ten times finer, and the analysis grid is refined automatically until it holds. The grid has a fixed number of points per half-power band of every resonance (one of them exactly at the natural frequency), so the check converges for a damping of 0.25 % as well as for one of 5 %; the numerical tolerance of the check (0.1 %) and the achieved ratio are shown with the result. If the table still exceeds the limit on the finest grid the verdict is FAIL and says what to change (widen the minimum notch width or lower the profile around the resonance). A probe or stress notch cannot re-evaluate the table between the frequencies of the harmonic run and gets no PASS. A zero profile or response, or a single frequency, gives no verdict. A structural damping of the study enters as the equivalent viscous ratio ζ = η/2. The result is the notch justification table (unnotched and notched input, reduction in dB, remaining margin) and a CSV export of the profile.
  • Bolts (ECSS) – preload range (tightening uncertainty, embedding, other losses), the thermal preload change from the differential expansion of the clamped parts and the bolt, gapping, slip, bolt tensile (yield, ultimate), shear, combined tension and shear, thread pull-out, crushing under the head / nut and hole bearing margins. The chain follows ECSS-E-HB-32-23A Rev.1 (6 February 2023); the clause of every formula is printed next to its margin (preload range 6.3.2.3 and 6.4, thermal 6.3.5, force ratio 7.4, gapping 7.8, tensile 7.9, pull-out 7.10, crushing 7.11, slip 9.2.3, shear and combined 9.3.2, hole bearing 9.3.4). A margin is capacity / demand − 1. J multiplies the external load only, never the preload (7.9.2; ECSS-E-ST-32-10C Table 4-4 note a), except in the slip, gapping, shear and hole bearing margins where the demand is an external load anyway. The thermally induced bolt load carries a factor too (ECSS-E-ST-32-10C 4.3.2.1 b with 4.3.2.2 a, 4.3.2.2 b, Table 4-4 notes a / e; no factor below 1,0 on an additive load, 4.3.2.1 d): by default each check applies its own J, but not less than 1,0, as JT to the thermal preload increment (the loss by the cold case in gapping and slip, the gain by the hot case in the tensile, combined, pull-out and crushing margins); the optional requirement-set row bolt_thermal_factor replaces it (1.0 is your decision, e.g. for an expendable launch vehicle, note e; a value below 1,0 is UNCONFIRMED). JT is printed in its own column of the table of the page and of the report. The handbook (6.3.14 – 6.3.18) adds the increment without a factor; the program is more conservative. J and every chain constant come from the requirement set; the allowables of the bolt and the part come from the confirmed A / B allowables of the study materials (the bolt needs Fsy for the yield margins of shear and combined – without it the result is UNCONFIRMED; the clamped part needs its yield strength Fy for the yield margins of crushing and hole bearing, which use the yield factor). The combined interaction is the square root of RA² + RS² (thread in the shear plane, the default) or RA² + RS³ (shank in the shear plane: input shear_plane in MCP). The thread pull-out uses the effective engagement Leng − 2 p and the thread-bending factors c1 c2 c3 (a nut: give its width across flats), and needs the shear allowables of both the bolt and the part; a nut whose width across flats / d is outside 1.4 – 1.9 leaves the pull-out UNCONFIRMED (c1 is only given in that range). With no axial force but a required clamp force FK,req the gapping margin is FV,min / FK,req − 1 and the joint FAILS when the minimum preload is below it; the joint FAILS also when the minimum preload FV,min (after the cold case) is not above zero, even with FK,req = 0 (the clamp force is gone). A negative minimum preload before the thermal change is taken as 0 and warned about. A prevailing torque (self-locking nut) lowers the minimum preload through the optional input F_nom_min (lowest nominal preload). A missing embedding (give 0 µm for none) or missing thermal data blocks a PASS; every direct input is validated (J > 0, 0 ≤ ρ < 1, 0 < µ ≤ 1, 0 < n ≤ 1, forces ≥ 0, grip length and engaged length > 0; a grip length that differs from the sum of the layers by more than 5 % is warned about). More than two friction interfaces per bolt need the confirmed requirement-set row bolt_slip_planes_max, otherwise the slip margin is UNCONFIRMED. For orientation only (not built in; enter them in the requirement set with their source): the handbook lists typical tightening uncertainties by method in Table 6-1 and typical embedding in Table 6-3. Deviations and limits of the program (all conservative or declared): the thermal increments are clipped at zero; shear, combined and hole bearing are computed for every joint although the handbook assigns them to bearing joints; the thread stress area is used as the shear area; a joint is assessed as one that must not gap; concentric joints only (no eccentric load introduction, chapter 8; no Young's modulus that changes with temperature, 6.3.5.3; no bending of the fastener, Table 5-1 note 1); not implemented: the tightening margin (6.5), the torque-preload relation (6.3.1), the slip check with the nominal preload of five or more bolts (9.2.2.2), the clearance strength of a bolt group (9.3.2.4) and the net-section and shear-out checks of the flanges (9.3.3, 9.3.5). The printed Eq. 6.3.28 of Rev.1 for several clamped materials has the opposite sign to Eq. 6.3.22; the program follows 6.3.22. The warnings of the calculation and the list of the deviations are shown under the table of the page and in the report. A stored bolt record of an older bolt model (field combined_exponent) is not recomputed: it gives a message to enter the bolt again; an old row bolt_combined_exponent in the requirement set is shown as obsolete and ignored.
  • PCB (Steinberg) – Zallow = c · B / (C · h · r · √L), the margin Zallow / (J · Znσ) − 1 and the three-band fatigue damage, cross-checked against the narrow-band damage of the spectral fatigue module. The constant c (with its unit: in, inch or mm), the amplitude convention, nσ, the component constant C and the lowest position factor rmin and the fatigue law are rows of the requirement set; a C or nσ typed into the page is used but never confirmed. The position factor is bounded from below (rused = max(r, rmin)); r and the RMS displacement are listed with their source: a typed r needs a source, a typed RMS displacement needs a source, and a typed value that differs from the one derived from the position (r) or from the model (RMS displacement) is UNCONFIRMED - typing r and the displacement alone cannot turn a failing board into a passing one. A zero response gives no verdict; a duration and a natural frequency without the fatigue rows of the requirement set give a warning and no fatigue verdict (never a silent PASS). The RMS relative displacement is typed or computed from the modal basis (board centre against its supports, the damping of the study); the cycle rate of the damage is √(m2/m0) of the response in the ASD band.
  • CubeSat rails – Apply supports writes the wall-normal supports on the outer faces of the rails and the axial support at the chosen rail end(s) into the study and stores the setup (part, kind, axis, sign) with it; every earlier rail support is removed first. Check the rails compares the support reactions and the lateral deflection with the limits and reports a wall or an end plate that would have to pull in any state (a pull below a relative numerical threshold of the total reaction is noise). Limitation of the linear model: two opposite supports - the walls of a lateral axis, the two end plates with both ends - share a load by halves and one of them pulls; a real wall or pusher plate cannot pull, so the margin uses the conservative force |push| + |pull of the opposite support| on the pushing side (exact for a rigid body between them, not a contact analysis; with a single held end the end that the load would pull away from is flagged). A rail without a support row gives no verdict.

The results are stored in the project file and appear in the Word report as subsections of the space chapter. A stored verdict is printed only while it is still true: it carries the digest of the requirement set (and whether it was confirmed), of the material allowables and of the study; when one of them changed the report says outdated - recompute, a record without these digests (an older or hand-edited file) is never PASS, and bolts, PCB and rails are recomputed from their stored inputs against the present requirement set and the report prints the recomputed rows; when the recomputation differs from the stored record (a hand edit, or a later program version) it says the stored result was not reproduced - recomputed values shown. A record whose stored inputs are missing (specification deleted, rail forces missing) gets no verdict. Notching and interface records cannot be recomputed (they depend on a modal analysis): the report prints stored result, not recomputed - tamper-evident only (staleness), not a signature next to them - the digests detect an outdated record, they do not authenticate it. The pages of the tab are cleared when a limit is edited or the dialog is reloaded.
